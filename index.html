Model fidelity & reference

All models are original procedural reconstructions of major subsystems. They are not dimensionally accurate CAD, complete bills of materials or exact depictions of proprietary internals. Engine-specific settings are illustrative simulation inputs; telemetry is not a manufacturer performance prediction.

UNDER THE HOOD

A model to learn with.

Quasi-steady, ideal-gas, isentropic nozzle flow. The supersonic area–Mach relation sets exit conditions; throat area and chamber pressure set choked mass flow.

F = ṁVₑ + (pₑ − pₐ)Aₑ
Isp = F / (ṁg₀)

Fuel pairs use fixed illustrative temperature, gas constant and heat-capacity ratio. Throttle scales chamber pressure with a 0.6 s response. Ambient pressure uses an exponential atmosphere.

The model excludes chemical equilibrium, heat transfer, pumps, structural limits, nozzle flow separation and combustion instability. Very overexpanded cases are outside its reliable range. This is an educational visualization, not an engine design tool.

EVENT LOG

A LITTLE THEORY. A LOT TO EXPLORE.

Three experiments to get you started.

01 / ENVIRONMENT

Take it to vacuum.

Ignite the engine, then increase altitude. Lower ambient pressure reduces the pressure penalty at the nozzle exit.

02 / GEOMETRY

Give the gas more room.

Increase nozzle expansion ratio. Exit velocity rises, but a large nozzle can lose performance at sea level.

03 / CONTROL

Dial back the thrust.

Reduce throttle during a burn. Watch chamber pressure, mass flow and thrust settle to their new values.
